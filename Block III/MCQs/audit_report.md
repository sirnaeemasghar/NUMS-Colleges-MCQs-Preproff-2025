## 3. Catalog of Flagged Items & Medical Discrepancies

### A. Critical Discrepancies (Medical Fact vs. Student PDF Key)

1. **CKMC-ANAT-06 (Page 17, Q6)**
   - *Question:* Hyperacusis with loss of taste indicates facial nerve lesion at —
   - *PDF Marked:* A. Stylomastoid foramen
   - *Proposed Verified:* C. Geniculate ganglion
   - *Clinical Rationale:* The nerve to stapedius (hyperacusis) and chorda tympani (taste) branch inside the facial canal *proximal* to the stylomastoid foramen. A lesion *at* the stylomastoid foramen causes purely motor facial paralysis, completely sparing taste and acoustic dampening. A lesion at or proximal to the geniculate ganglion knocks out all distal branches.
   - *Source:* Snell's Clinical Anatomy by Regions, 10th ed., Chapter 11.

2. **CKMC-ANAT-22 (Page 20, Q22)**
   - *Question:* The lingual artery runs —
   - *PDF Marked:* A. Over hyoglossus *(Note: Handwritten circle around B in document)*
   - *Proposed Verified:* B. Under hyoglossus
   - *Clinical Rationale:* The lingual artery runs *deep to (under)* the hyoglossus muscle. In contrast, the lingual nerve, hypoglossal nerve (CN XII), and deep lingual vein run *superficial to (over)* the hyoglossus. The PDF checkmark was placed on A, but a student annotated and circled B on the physical exam paper.
   - *Source:* Snell's Clinical Anatomy by Regions, 10th ed., Chapter 11 (Submandibular Region); BD Chaurasia Vol 3.

3. **QIMS-PHYS-44 (Pages 62–63, Q44)**
   - *Question:* A 46-year-old man has "puffy" skin and is lethargic. His plasma TSH concentration is low and increases markedly when he is given TRH. Most likely diagnosis?
   - *PDF Marked:* d. Hypothyroidism due to an abnormality in the pituitary
   - *Proposed Verified:* c. Hypothyroidism due to an abnormality in the hypothalamus
   - *Clinical Rationale:* If exogenous TRH causes a robust increase in TSH, the pituitary thyrotrophs are fully functioning and responsive. The deficiency is due to lack of hypothalamic TRH secretion (tertiary hypothyroidism). If the defect were intrinsic to the pituitary (secondary hypothyroidism), thyrotrophs would fail to respond to TRH.
   - *Source:* Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed., Chapter 77; Harrison's Endocrinology.

4. **QIMS-PHYS-55 (Page 64, Q55)**
   - *Question:* A young woman is given daily injections of a substance beginning on day 16... as long as injections continue, she does not menstruate. The injected substance could be:
   - *PDF Marked:* c. An inhibitor of progesterone’s actions
   - *Proposed Verified:* d. HCG
   - *Clinical Rationale:* Progesterone is essential to maintain the secretory endometrium; an inhibitor of progesterone actions (e.g. mifepristone) causes immediate endometrial breakdown and menses! To prevent menstruation, either hCG (which rescues the corpus luteum) or exogenous progesterone must be administered. The PDF key marked (c), which is biologically inverted.
   - *Source:* Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed., Chapter 82 & 83.

5. **QIMS-PHYS-56 (Pages 64–65, Q56)**
   - *Question:* A professional athlete in her mid-20s has not had a menstrual cycle for 5 years, although a bone density scan revealed normal skeletal mineralization. Which fact may explain these observations?
   - *PDF Marked:* c. Her plasma estrogen concentration is very low
   - *Proposed Verified:* d. She has been taking anabolic steroid supplements for 5 years
   - *Clinical Rationale:* Five years of athletic amenorrhea with very low estrogen invariably produces significant osteopenia/osteoporosis (female athlete triad). The preservation of normal bone mineral density despite prolonged amenorrhea points to anabolic androgenic steroid use, which suppresses the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis while strongly stimulating osteoblasts.
   - *Source:* Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology, 14th ed., Chapter 81 & 82.
